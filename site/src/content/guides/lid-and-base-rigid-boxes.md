A lid-and-base rigid box is a two-piece paper box with a separate top lid and bottom base. It is one of the most common structures for premium gift boxes because it feels simple, strong and gift-ready.

Use this structure when the packaging needs to look premium before the product is opened.

## Good use cases

Lid-and-base rigid boxes work well for:

- Gift sets
- Candle sets
- Jewelry packaging
- Perfume and fragrance kits
- Skincare launch boxes
- Premium accessories
- Retail presentation boxes
- Ecommerce products with a gift-ready unboxing

They are less useful when unit cost, flat shipping or very low freight volume matters more than presentation.

## What to check before sampling

| Detail | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Lid height | Changes the opening feel and how much of the base is covered |
| Base depth | Must match product height and insert layout |
| Insert height | Keeps products visible and stable inside the box |
| Board thickness | Affects strength, weight and premium feel |
| Paper wrap | Changes color, texture and print result |
| Finish placement | Foil, embossing or UV should be planned before sampling |

## Inserts are often the real structure

For gift sets, the outer box is only part of the job. The insert decides how the product sits, what customers see first and whether items move during shipping.

Common insert options include paperboard inserts, wrapped paperboard inserts, molded pulp, EVA and foam. Paper inserts are often worth testing first when the product is not too heavy or fragile.

## Freight planning

Lid-and-base rigid boxes usually do not ship flat. That means freight and storage can become a large part of the landed cost.

Ask for packed carton size, gross weight and units per carton before comparing quotes. This is especially important if you are shipping assembled gift boxes from China.
