The MOQ for custom printed boxes depends on the box style, material, printing method, surface finish and destination. A simple folding carton usually has a different minimum from a rigid box, gift box or custom corrugated mailer.

If you are launching a new product, ask for a pilot quantity and a bulk quantity in the same message. That gives you a realistic view of setup cost, unit cost and shipping cost before you commit.

## Why MOQ changes

Custom packaging has setup work before production starts. The supplier may need a dieline, printing setup, paper cutting, proofing, lamination, foil stamping or insert preparation. These steps cost time even when the order is small.

| Box type | Why the minimum can be different |
| --- | --- |
| Folding cartons | Lighter paperboard, efficient cutting and flat shipping often make them easier for small runs. |
| Mailer boxes | Corrugated board, larger size and print setup can raise the practical minimum. |
| Rigid boxes | More handwork, board wrapping and insert planning usually make small runs less economical. |
| Gift sets | Several parts may be involved: outer box, insert, sleeve, ribbon or printed tissue. |

## What to send first

Send the box style, size, quantity, material preference, printing requirement and destination country. If you do not know the material yet, send a product photo and a reference box.

Useful quote details:

- Product size and weight
- Box style or reference photo
- Target quantity
- Printing outside only or inside and outside
- Finish requirements such as matte lamination, foil stamping or embossing
- Destination country and deadline

## How small brands should ask

Ask for two quantities: a smaller pilot run and a better-value bulk run. This makes the cost difference clear without guessing.

For example, instead of asking only "What is your MOQ?", ask: "Please quote 500 pieces and 2,000 pieces for this mailer box, shipped to the United States." That gives the supplier enough context to answer usefully.

## When a low MOQ is not the cheapest option

A lower quantity can reduce total cash outlay, but it may increase unit cost. Printing setup, sampling, cutting and packing are spread across fewer boxes. If the product will be reordered, a larger first run can sometimes be more practical.

## FAQ

### Can I order samples before bulk production?

Yes. A sample is useful for checking box size, structure, print color and finish before bulk production.

### Does MOQ change when I add foil or embossing?

It can. Foil stamping, embossing, spot UV and special paper add setup work, so the practical minimum may change.
