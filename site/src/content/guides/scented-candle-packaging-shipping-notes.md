Shipping candles internationally requires two separate plans: protect the candle and jar from physical damage, then confirm the route, labels and documents with your carrier, freight forwarder, customs broker or receiving warehouse.

ZiiBoxes can help with the packaging, pack-out, carton details and China-side handoff. Your logistics provider should confirm the final country, carrier, customs and product requirements for the specific shipment.

## International candle shipping risks to plan for

| Risk | What can happen | Packaging response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glass vessel | Chipping, cracking or breakage | Control movement and separate glass from hard edges |
| Heat exposure | Wax softening, sweating or surface changes | Reduce avoidable heat exposure and confirm route timing |
| Heavy product | Insert collapse or carton failure | Match insert and carton strength to packed weight |
| Scented product | Route may request clearer product information | Give the logistics provider accurate product details |
| Mixed gift set | Items collide or SKUs are confused | Use dividers and clear pack-out instructions |
| Long freight route | More handling and stacking | Test the complete retail and export pack together |

Do not solve only the most visible risk. A strong retail box can still fail when several heavy jars are packed into a weak master carton.

## Use an inner pack and an outer shipping pack

The retail candle box supports presentation, branding and local protection. The outer shipper or master carton handles stacking, transport and repeated movement.

A single candle shipped directly to a customer may use a fitted retail box inside a corrugated shipping carton with suitable clearance and cushioning. Multiple retail units moving by freight should be arranged inside a master carton with dividers, pads or another tested packing method.

FedEx advises choosing a sturdy outer box, cushioning contents and preventing movement inside the package. Its [packing guidance](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/packing.html) is a useful general reference, but your carrier may set different requirements.

## Control jar movement inside the candle box

Measure the finished jar at its widest points, including the lid. Record the product weight and check whether the base, shoulder or lid creates a pressure point.

The insert should hold the vessel without making packing difficult. Paperboard, corrugated, molded pulp and foam-based solutions each behave differently. Start with the [candle box insert guide](/guides/candle-box-inserts-protection/) and test the chosen material with the real filled candle.

For glass vessels, avoid direct contact between jars in a set. A divider should stay in position when the box is lifted, turned and handled. The [glass jar candle protection guide](/guides/glass-jar-candle-packaging-protection/) covers the full inner-to-outer packaging system.

## Plan for heat without promising temperature control

Wax can react to prolonged heat, direct sun or hot vehicles. Packaging cannot guarantee a stable temperature through an uncontrolled international route.

Practical steps include:

- Ask the logistics provider about typical transit time and storage conditions.
- Avoid leaving packed candles near heat sources before collection.
- Use a closure or inner wrap that keeps dust away if the wax surface softens.
- Choose seasonal shipping windows and faster services when heat risk justifies the cost.
- Test the product after realistic warm storage when your candle formula is sensitive.

The candle maker should confirm how the specific wax, fragrance load, color and vessel behave. Insulated solutions may be discussed for a defined route, but they need route-specific testing and do not provide unlimited protection.

## Test the complete packed shipment

Test the final product, retail pack, shipping pack and master carton together. A sample box with an empty jar does not represent the weight or behavior of a finished candle.

ISTA publishes packaged-product test procedures for different distribution situations. Review the available [ISTA test procedures](https://ista.org/test_procedures.php) with your test provider, carrier or logistics partner to choose a suitable approach rather than selecting a test name by guesswork.

Record the packed weight, insert version, carton material and packing pattern used for the approved test. A later material or jar change may require another check.

## Prepare master cartons and pallet details

Heavy candles can make a carton difficult to lift before the carton looks full. Set both a unit count and a carton-weight limit.

Confirm:

- Retail units per master carton
- Product orientation and divider pattern
- Carton dimensions and gross weight
- Carton marks and SKU identification
- Pallet pattern, height and wrap when palletized
- Total cartons, units and gross weight

Keep the carton count consistent with the packing list. The [freight forwarder carton guide](/guides/freight-forwarder-carton-details/) explains the details normally prepared after packing.

## Confirm product information, labels and documents

Tell your logistics provider that the shipment contains finished scented candles, the vessel material, approximate product composition when requested, unit count, value and destination. Do not describe the goods only as “gifts” or “home products” if more detail is required.

Some routes, marketplaces and warehouses may request product or safety information. The responsible seller, manufacturer and logistics provider should confirm what applies. For Amazon-bound stock, use the [Amazon dangerous goods paperwork notes](/guides/amazon-dangerous-goods-packaging-paperwork/) as a starting checklist, not as a substitute for Amazon's current decision.

## Common mistakes when shipping candles internationally

- Testing an empty jar instead of a filled, finished candle.
- Relying on the retail gift box as the only shipping protection.
- Allowing glass jars to touch inside a multi-candle set.
- Ignoring total master-carton weight.
- Assuming insulation guarantees protection from summer heat.
- Printing carton marks before the receiving route confirms its format.
- Booking freight before product details and documents are reviewed.

## FAQ

### Can candles be shipped internationally?

Often yes, but the answer depends on the product, destination and selected carrier or freight route. Give the logistics provider accurate product details and ask it to confirm current requirements before booking.

### How do you protect glass candle jars during shipping?

Use a fitted insert or cushioning system that controls movement, keep glass items separated and place the retail pack inside a suitable outer shipping pack. Test the complete filled product.

### Will an insulated box stop candles from melting?

It may slow heat transfer for a limited period, but it does not guarantee temperature control through an unknown route. Product behavior, outside temperature, storage and transit time all matter.

### Does a candle gift box need an outer carton?

Usually yes when it will move through parcel or freight handling. A gift box is designed mainly for presentation and local protection unless the complete shipping system has been tested for the route.

### What carton details are needed for candle freight?

Prepare units per carton, carton dimensions, gross weight, carton count, SKU details, marks and pallet information when applicable. Confirm the final format with the freight provider or warehouse.
