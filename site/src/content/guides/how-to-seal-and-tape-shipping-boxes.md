You just finished packing a batch of ecommerce orders. The corrugated boxes are filled, the inserts are holding your products in place, and the shipping labels are printed. But before you hand these parcels off to the mail carrier, you face a critical step: sealing the boxes. If you choose the wrong tape or apply it incorrectly, a box can burst open on a conveyor belt, leaving your products damaged or lost.

The practical answer is straightforward. Use pressure-sensitive plastic tape or water-activated paper tape that is at least 2 to 3 inches wide, and apply it across all flaps and seams using the H-tape method. Major carriers like [USPS](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm) and [FedEx](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/packing.html) have specific guidelines for how they expect packages to be sealed. Following these rules helps your packages survive automated sorting machines and transit vibrations without failing.

Here is how you can select the right materials and apply the correct box sealing methods for your brand.

## Why Proper Box Sealing Matters for US Carriers

When you send a package through a major carrier, it enters an automated network. Packages travel on conveyor belts, slide down chutes, and share space with items that can weigh up to 70 pounds. Your box needs to hold together under these conditions.

Carrier packaging requirements exist to protect your products and the people handling them. If a box breaks open because the tape failed, the carrier might deny a damage claim. The package could be delayed, returned to your facility, or lost entirely. [USPS advises shippers to use strong packaging that does not bulge](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm) and to use enough packing tape to close and secure all seams. 

Properly sealing shipping boxes keeps your products secure and helps you avoid unexpected fees or claim denials.

## How to Choose the Right Packing Tape for Shipping Boxes

Selecting the right tape is just as important as choosing the right box. You need a tape that bonds securely to corrugated cardboard and can handle temperature changes during transit.

### Pressure-Sensitive Plastic Tape

This is the standard packing tape most ecommerce brands use. It is easy to apply with a desktop tape dispenser. FedEx approves pressure-sensitive plastic tape as long as it is at least 3 inches wide. USPS requires a minimum width of 2 inches. To satisfy both carriers, buy tape that is at least 2 or 3 inches wide.

### Water-Activated Paper Tape

Water-activated tape requires a specialized dispenser that wets the adhesive as you dispense the tape. When the wet adhesive dries, it penetrates the corrugated cardboard fibers, creating a strong, permanent bond. FedEx specifically lists water-activated paper tape and reinforced water-activated tape as approved materials. This tape works well for heavier boxes and provides a tamper-evident seal.

### What to Avoid

Do not use masking tape, scotch tape, or electrical tape. These materials do not have the shear strength required to hold corrugated flaps together under stress. Also, avoid strapping your boxes with string or twine, which can jam automated sorting equipment.

## Box Sealing Methods: The H-Tape Technique

The H-tape method is the standard technique approved by carriers. The goal is to cover every seam on the top and bottom of the box so no raw edges are exposed.

### Step-by-Step H-Tape Guide

1. **Center seam:** Run a strip of tape down the center seam where the two main flaps meet. 
2. **Edge seams:** Place a strip of tape over the two side seams where the smaller flaps meet the larger ones.
3. **Reinforce if needed:** For heavier boxes, apply a second layer of tape over the center seam.

When you finish the top and bottom, the tape pattern forms a letter H. [FedEx instructs shippers to use the H-taping method](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/packing.html) on both the top and bottom of the outer box.

## Sealing Shipping Boxes for Different Parcel Types

Different products require different approaches to box preparation and sealing. If your items are prone to breakage, you should review our [fragile product packaging shipping protection](/guides/fragile-product-packaging-shipping-protection/) guide before sealing.

### Standard Ecommerce Orders

For a standard B-flute mailer box, a single strip of 2-inch or 3-inch pressure-sensitive tape across the center seam is often enough. If you are shipping a heavier item, apply the full H-tape pattern to prevent the bottom from giving way.

### Heavy or Overweight Parcels

If you are shipping dense items like glass jars or heavy ceramics, the box needs extra reinforcement. Use reinforced water-activated tape for a stronger hold. Apply the H-tape method on both ends, and consider adding an extra strip of tape across the side seams.

### International Shipments

International packages pass through more handling points and face varying humidity levels. Use water-activated reinforced tape to ensure the seal holds up during long transit times. Make sure all old labels and barcodes are completely covered or blacked out if you are reusing a box.

## Box Sealing and Preparation Checklist

Use this checklist before you hand your packages off to a carrier:

- [ ] Box is sturdy and free of damage or punctures.
- [ ] 2 to 3 inches of cushioning surrounds the product inside the box.
- [ ] Flaps close flush without bulging.
- [ ] Tape is at least 2 inches wide (3 inches preferred for FedEx).
- [ ] H-tape method applied to both the top and bottom seams.
- [ ] No string, cord, or twine wrapped around the box.
- [ ] Old shipping labels and barcodes are removed or fully covered.
- [ ] New shipping label placed flat on the largest surface, not folded over edges.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced shippers make mistakes that can lead to damaged products. Watch out for these common pitfalls when sealing shipping boxes.

### Using the Wrong Tape Width

Using 1-inch tape might save a little money on supplies, but it does not provide enough surface area to hold heavy flaps together. Stick to 2-inch or 3-inch tape to meet carrier requirements.

### Failing to Tape the Bottom Correctly

Many shippers focus on the top of the box and simply fold the bottom flaps without enough tape. If the bottom fails, the product falls out. Always apply the H-tape method to the bottom of the box first.

### Leaving Old Labels Visible

If you reuse a box, old barcodes and shipping labels can confuse automated scanners. [USPS warns shippers to make sure all old logos, barcodes, shipping labels, and addresses are covered or blacked out](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm). 

### Wrapping Boxes in String or Twine

Some shippers try to add extra security by tying string around a box. Do not do this. String and twine catch on conveyor belts and sorting equipment, which can rip the box open or jam the machinery.

### Taping Over the Label Unintentionally

While you can use clear packing tape to secure a printed shipping label to the box, you must ensure the tape is smooth and does not bend or fold the label. [USPS advises placing all labels on the same side and not bending or overlapping them](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm). Bubbled or wrinkled tape over a barcode can cause scan failures.

## FAQ

### What is the H-tape method for shipping boxes?
The H-tape method involves applying packing tape across the center seam and the two side seams on both the top and bottom of a box. The pattern forms a letter H and covers all raw edges. FedEx requires this method for all box shipments.

### Can I use regular tape to seal shipping boxes?
No, you should not use regular tape like scotch tape or masking tape. Carriers require pressure-sensitive plastic tape or water-activated tape that is at least 2 to 3 inches wide to ensure the box stays closed during transit.

### Do carriers provide free shipping boxes?
Yes, USPS offers free Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express boxes, and FedEx offers complimentary boxes for account holders using expedited services. However, if you use these specific boxes, you must pay for the corresponding premium shipping service.

### Can I reuse an old box for shipping?
Yes, you can reuse a sturdy box as long as it has not been crushed, torn, or punctured. You must remove or completely cover all old shipping labels, barcodes, and addresses to prevent sorting errors.

### Why can I not wrap my shipping box in string?
String, cord, and twine get caught in automated sorting equipment. This can damage the machinery and tear your box open. Carriers like USPS specifically prohibit wrapping boxes in these materials.
