You ordered a batch of custom mailer boxes for your ecommerce brand. The print looks perfect, the structure feels solid in your hands, and the inserts hold your products exactly where they should. You ship the first batch of orders, and within a week, customer emails start coming in. Boxes are arriving crushed, corners are split, and the products inside are scratched or broken. Your packaging looked great on a desk, but it failed the realities of the parcel delivery network. This is a common scenario for brands that skip package performance testing.

The practical answer is to validate your packaging before you commit to a full production run. By running ISTA transit testing packaging evaluations, you can find and fix weak points in your boxes, inserts, and sealing methods before they reach your customers. The International Safe Transit Association (ISTA) develops test procedures that simulate the shocks, vibrations, and drops a package experiences during shipping. If your boxes pass these tests, you have verifiable proof that your packaging will hold up in the real world.

Here is how to prepare your custom packaging for transit testing, from understanding the relevant test series to fixing common points of failure.

## Understanding ISTA Transit Testing and Test Series

ISTA is a global organization that creates standards for how packaged products should perform during distribution. Their tests range from basic screening tools used early in the design process to general simulations of specific shipment types. The goal of ISTA transit testing is not to test the product itself, but to test the combined system of your product and the packaging around it.

When you send a package to a testing lab, they subject it to a sequence of hazards. This usually includes impact drops, vibration on a shaking table, and sometimes atmospheric conditioning like extreme temperatures or humidity. The lab follows a specific procedure, and you receive a report detailing whether the package survived without product damage. If you are new to this process, review the [ISTA test procedures](https://ista.org/test_procedures.php) to understand your options before contacting a lab.

ISTA categorizes its tests into different series based on how much you want to simulate the real world. You need to choose the series that matches your actual shipping method. The 1-Series are integrity tests that challenge the strength and robustness of the product and package combination. They are useful as screening tests early in the design process. The 2-Series adds partial simulation, including elements like atmospheric conditioning.

The 3-Series provides a laboratory simulation of the damage-producing motions, forces, and conditions of transport environments. If you ship individual parcels through a delivery service, you will likely look at Procedure 3A, which covers parcel delivery system shipments of 150 lbs or less. The 6-Series includes tests created by or in cooperation with specific retailers to reflect their unique supply chain conditions. For example, if you are a vendor shipping to Amazon Fulfillment Centers, you might need to pass Project 6-AMAZON.COM-SIOC. This tests products shipped in their own container without additional packaging by Amazon.

## How to Prepare Your Package for the Lab

Preparing for drop testing packaging evaluations requires more than just bringing an empty box to a facility. You need to replicate exactly what your customer will receive.

### Selecting Your Test Samples
Send the actual production samples you intend to use. Do not send hand-made prototypes if your mass-produced boxes will be machine-folded. The lab needs to test the same materials, glue patterns, and structural folds that will ship to your customers. You should also send multiple samples, as labs typically test several identical packages to ensure consistency.

### Packing the Product Exactly as Shipped
Place your actual product inside the box, along with any inserts, void fill, or padding you plan to use. If your product includes a glass jar, put the actual jar in the box. If you use a paper insert to separate two bottles, include that insert. The lab cannot accurately test shipping box integrity if the internal mass and support structure are missing.

### Sealing the Package Correctly
How you seal the box matters just as much as the box structure. Use the exact tape and sealing method you will use in production. FedEx recommends using pressure-sensitive plastic tape, water-activated paper tape, or reinforced tape that is at least 3 inches wide, applied using the H taping method across all [seams](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/packing.html). The H taping method involves applying tape to the center seam and both edge seams, forming an H shape. The lab will not fix your sealing mistakes before testing, so prepare the boxes exactly as they will leave your warehouse.

## What Happens During Package Performance Testing

When the lab receives your package, they follow the chosen ISTA procedure step by step. Understanding what they do helps you know what to expect in the final report.

### Drop Testing and Impact Sequences
The lab will drop your packaged product from specific heights onto different faces, edges, and corners. The drop height depends on the weight of the package and the specific test standard. Lighter packages are dropped from higher heights, simulating how individuals handle smaller boxes. This tests the crush resistance of your box corners and whether your internal padding keeps the product from hitting the inside of the carton.

### Vibration and Compression Testing
Packages ride on trucks and conveyor belts, which subject them to constant vibration. The lab places your package on a vibration table that simulates the random vibrations of a truck suspension system. They may also apply a compression force to see if the box buckles when stacked under other packages during transit. If your master carton details are off, compression testing will reveal the weak link.

## Common Weak Points Revealed by Shipping Box Testing

When packages fail transit testing, the failures usually happen in a few predictable areas. Knowing these before you test can help you design better packaging from the start.

### Corner and Edge Failure
Corrugated boxes get their strength from their vertical corners. If a drop test splits the corner of your mailer box, the product inside absorbs the impact. This often happens when the glue tab is too narrow or the corrugated board is too thin for the product weight. If your box fails at the corners, you may need to look at [mailer boxes vs shipping boxes](/guides/mailer-boxes-vs-shipping-boxes/) to see if you are using the wrong structure for your product's weight.

### Product Shift and Internal Damage
Sometimes the outer box looks perfect, but the product inside is broken. This means your internal void fill or paper inserts failed to lock the product in place. When a box is dropped, the product will keep moving until it hits something solid. If your insert allows movement, the product will slam into the inside of the box. You need to compare paper inserts, plastic trays, and other insert options to find the one that locks your specific product securely in place.

## Fixing Failures and Re-Testing Your Packaging

If your packaging fails the ISTA test, do not panic. The purpose of lab testing is to find these failures before they cost you customer refunds and negative reviews. You will receive a report detailing exactly where the failure occurred.

If the outer box failed, you may need to increase the corrugated board grade or adjust the flute type. Flute type refers to the arches of paper inside the corrugated board, which come in different sizes. If the product shifted, you need to redesign the insert. Sometimes adding a simple cardboard pad or changing the orientation of the product inside the box makes a significant difference. Making these adjustments is much cheaper before you finalize a production order.

Once you make changes to the box structure or insert design, order new samples and send them back to the lab. You should re-run the same ISTA procedure to verify the fix worked. Do not assume a change will pass without testing it. The goal is to reach a point where the lab confirms the packaging passes the chosen procedure without product damage.

## Comparison and Checklist for Transit Testing

Use this table to help identify which test series might apply to your situation.

| ISTA Series | What It Tests | Typical Application | Predictive Ability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-Series | Integrity and robustness | Early design screening | Low, useful as a consistent benchmark |
| 2-Series | Partial simulation | Refining preliminary designs | Moderate, includes some environmental factors |
| 3-Series | General simulation | Parcel, LTL, FTL, and international shipping | High, simulates actual transport hazards |
| 6-Series | Member performance | Retailer-specific supply chains (e.g., Amazon) | High, reflects specific distribution conditions |

Before you contact a testing lab or request a packaging quote, gather the following specification information so you can order the right samples.

- Exact external dimensions of the finished box
- Type of material (e.g., corrugated board, paperboard, rigid chipboard)
- Board thickness or caliper
- Flute type, if using corrugated materials
- Type and placement of internal inserts or dividers
- The exact product that will go inside the box, including its weight and dimensions
- The sealing method and tape width you will use
- The target shipping method (e.g., individual parcel, LTL, or full truckload)
- The specific retailer requirements, if shipping to a marketplace like Amazon

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Brands often waste time and money on testing because they make simple preparation errors. Avoid these common mistakes.

First, do not test an empty box. The mass of the product changes how the box reacts to vibration and drops. An empty box will perform differently than a loaded box. You must send the package with the actual product inside.

Second, do not use old or degraded samples. If you grab a box that has been sitting in your office for six months, the paper fibers may have dried out and become brittle. Use fresh samples directly from your manufacturer's production run.

Third, do not ignore the internal filling. As [USPS notes](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm) when advising customers on how to prepare and send a package, you should use strong packaging with enough room to add cushioning to prevent items from shifting. Your package will share space with other packages that could weigh as much as 70 lbs. If you leave empty space inside the box, it will fail the compression test. You can avoid this by planning your [shipping carton size](/guides/shipping-carton-size-freight-cost/) carefully to minimize void space.

Fourth, do not use the wrong tape. A strong box sealed with weak tape will burst open during a drop test. Use a tape that is at least 3 inches wide and apply it using the H taping method across all seams.

Fifth, do not forget to confirm your [master carton details for Amazon FBA](/guides/master-carton-details-amazon-fba/) if you are testing unit loads. If your individual boxes pass but your master carton fails, the individual boxes will still get crushed when stacked on a pallet.

## Making a Decision on Your ISTA Transit Testing Packaging

Transit testing is an investment in your brand's reputation. When a customer receives a damaged product, they do not blame the shipping carrier. They blame your brand. By preparing your packaging for ISTA transit testing, you take the guesswork out of your structural design. You can confidently launch a new product, switch box suppliers, or change your insert material knowing that your packaging will survive the journey.

Start by identifying your shipping method. If you ship individual parcels, look into a 3A test. If you ship to a specific retailer, check their requirements. Order your samples, pack them exactly as they will ship, seal them properly, and send them to an accredited lab. If they pass, you are ready for production. If they fail, make the adjustments and test again. When you are ready to produce your tested boxes, [request a quote](/request-a-quote/) for your custom packaging.

## FAQ

### What does ISTA testing stand for?
ISTA stands for the International Safe Transit Association. They create test procedures that simulate the shocks, vibrations, drops, and compression forces a package experiences during shipping to ensure the product and package combination survives transit.

### How much does ISTA transit testing cost?
Testing costs depend on the specific test series you choose, the number of samples you send, and the lab you use. You must contact an accredited testing lab directly for a quote. Costs vary widely based on the complexity of the test.

### Do I need to put my actual product inside the box for testing?
Yes, you must test the package with the actual product inside. The weight, shape, and rigidity of the product change how the box reacts to drops and vibrations. An empty box will not give you accurate results.

### What is the difference between ISTA 1-Series and 3-Series tests?
The 1-Series are non-simulation integrity tests used as basic screening tools early in the design process. The 3-Series are general simulation tests that closely simulate the actual damage-producing motions, forces, and conditions of a specific transport environment.

### Can I use any tape to seal my package for testing?
No, you should use the exact tape and sealing method you plan to use in mass production. Labs recommend using pressure-sensitive plastic tape or water-activated reinforced tape that is at least 3 inches wide, applied evenly across all flaps and seams.
