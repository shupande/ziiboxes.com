You just received a sample of your custom packaging from your supplier, and the artwork looks exactly right. You apply your UPC label, ship the units to a fulfillment center, and wait. Then you get the notification: a large portion of your inventory is stranded because the barcodes will not scan. The problem was not the label printer. It was the placement, the sizing, or the contrast of the barcode against the printed box design.

The practical answer is simple: follow established GS1 standards for quiet zones, sizing, and color contrast before you finalize your artwork. When you plan your barcode placement packaging early, you avoid retailer chargebacks, fulfillment delays, and manual relabeling costs.

## How GS1 Standards Affect Your Custom Packaging

GS1 is the global organization that develops and maintains the standards for barcodes used in retail and supply chains. When a retailer or a fulfillment center scans a product, their scanners expect the barcode to meet specific GS1 parameters. If your custom packaging ignores these parameters, the scanner cannot read the code.

According to [GS1 US barcode printing guidelines](https://www.gs1us.org/upcs-barcodes-prefixes/how-to-use-your-upc-barcodes/place-barcodes-on-products), the quality of your barcode is critical for a successful scan. A barcode can look perfect on a printed box but still fail at checkout. If it fails, the retailer has to manually key the GTIN (Global Trade Item Number), which can lead to lost sales and retailer chargebacks. To ensure scannability, you need high-resolution artwork and high-resolution printing so the lines and spaces remain clean and readable.

## Barcode Placement on Packaging: Location Rules

Where you place the barcode on your custom box directly impacts how easily a scanner can read it. The physical surface, the location on the package, and the surrounding design elements all matter.

### Choosing a Flat, Scannable Surface

The printed surface must be smooth so nothing interferes with the scanner. Avoid placing barcodes over box folds, creases, embossed logos, or heavy texture lines. If you are using a rigid box with a thick lid edge, do not let the barcode wrap over the lid hinge. Keep it entirely on a single, flat panel.

### Standard Location Guidelines

For an item scanned at checkout, the best practice is to place the barcode in the lower right-hand section of the back of the package. You should avoid the extreme edge of the package. Allowing enough white space surrounding the barcode helps ensure a clean scan ([GS1 US](https://www.gs1us.org/upcs-barcodes-prefixes/how-to-use-your-upc-barcodes/place-barcodes-on-products)).

There are additional guidelines for bagged items, large or bulky items, curved items, and items scanned in warehouses or distribution centers. If your product sits in a clear bag rather than a paper box, you need to plan the label placement so the bag lies flat against the product when scanned.

### Placement Considerations for Amazon FBA

If you sell on Amazon, your packaging must meet specific prep requirements. Every unit requires an accessible, scannable barcode. Additionally, any existing external barcodes on shipping boxes must be covered or rendered unscannable to prevent processing errors ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a6a5bbb9-3d5e-4ec1-afb6-e1459dd810f6?postId=a6a5bbb9-3d5e-4ec1-afb6-e1459dd810f6)).

If you print directly on your paper box, ensure the primary FNSKU or UPC sits on a flat, uncoated panel. If you use glossy spot UV over the barcode area, the reflection can interfere with the laser. ## UPC Label Size and Barcode Sizing Rules

The size of your barcode depends on where you will use the product and how you will print the barcode. UPC barcodes have a fixed relationship between height and width. When you modify one dimension, you must alter the other by a proportional amount.

### Why You Should Never Truncate a Barcode

It is best to print barcodes at their full height. Sometimes designers want to shorten the barcode to make it fit a sleek box design. This practice, called truncation, can result in incorrect scanning. A truncated barcode might look fine to a human eye, but a scanner relies on the full height to read the data accurately. If the height is cut, the scanner might misread the code entirely.

### Resizing Artwork Correctly

Create your barcode art in the exact size you need for your packaging. If you need to change the size of the artwork, do not manually shrink or enlarge it in your design software. Stretching or compressing the image distorts the width of the bars and spaces, meaning it may not scan correctly. If you need a different size, regenerate the barcode file in the desired size using a GS1 tool or a certified provider ([GS1 US](https://www.gs1us.org/upcs-barcodes-prefixes/how-to-use-your-upc-barcodes/place-barcodes-on-products)).

When preparing your artwork files for the printer, ensure the barcode vector file is included and clearly labeled. Our [artwork file checklist for custom boxes](/guides/artwork-file-checklist/) covers exactly what to prepare before sending your files to a supplier.

## Understanding the GS1 Quiet Zone

The quiet zone is the clear, unprinted area on the left and right sides of the barcode. Scanners need this blank space to identify the start and end of the barcode data. If you place text, graphic elements, or a border too close to the barcode, the scanner cannot read it.

GS1 standards dictate minimum widths for these quiet zones. The exact width depends on the barcode type, but a general rule is to leave a solid white margin that is at least ten times the width of the narrowest bar in the code. When you send artwork to your packaging supplier, explicitly tell them not to print crop marks, registration marks, or border lines through the quiet zone.

## Barcode Color and Contrast Guidelines

Color choices can make or break a barcode. The best and safest color combination is black bars printed on a white background. If you want to use other colors to match your brand, you must follow specific color rules.

### Selecting Bar and Background Colors

GS1 barcodes require dark colors for the bars. Acceptable options include black, dark blue, or dark green. You must avoid printing the bars in red or a reddish color like brown. Barcode scanners use red light, which means red bars are invisible to the scanner.

For the background, GS1 requires light colors for the quiet zones and spaces. You can also use reddish backgrounds because the scanner light is red and cannot read the red background. Typically, you would not print a background color behind the barcode at all. Instead, the background is simply the color of the paper material you choose for your box.

### Avoiding Common Print Issues

If you do decide to print a solid background behind the barcode, you must ensure full ink coverage. If you use multiple layers of ink to increase opacity, print each layer as a solid. If you use a fine screen to deliver more ink, make sure there are no voids or gaps where the white paper shows through. Any inconsistency in the background can interfere with the scan ([GS1 US](https://www.gs1us.org/upcs-barcodes-prefixes/how-to-use-your-upc-barcodes/place-barcodes-on-products)).

## Barcode Printing Guidelines Comparison

Use this comparison to understand how different choices affect scannability when planning your custom packaging.

## Barcode Specification Checklist

Before you send your custom box artwork to a supplier like ZiiBoxes, run through this checklist to ensure your barcode will scan on the first try.

*   **Barcode type identified:** Confirm whether you need a UPC-A, EAN-13, or FNSKU, and use the correct file format.
*   **Quiet zone verified:** Ensure clear white space surrounds the left and right sides of the barcode.
*   **Placement confirmed:** The barcode sits on a flat, unprinted panel on the back of the box.
*   **Size maintained:** The barcode is printed at full height without truncation.
*   **Colors checked:** The bars are dark (black, dark blue, dark green) and the background is light or unprinted.
*   **Spot UV avoided:** No glossy coatings, lamination, or spot UV cover the barcode area.
*   **Artwork resolved:** The barcode is a high-resolution vector file, not a resized JPEG.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced product managers make barcode errors during the rush of a packaging launch. Watch out for these common issues:

### Placing Barcodes on Curved or Peeling Surfaces

Do not place a barcode where it will bend around a corner or curve. If you apply a label to a round bottle or a narrow mailer box edge, the barcode warps. Scanners cannot read distorted barcodes. Always find a flat surface that is at least wide enough to hold the entire barcode and its quiet zones.

### Ignoring Spot UV and Coating Overlaps

Spot UV looks impressive on a logo, but it creates a reflective glare over the barcode if the design overlaps. Printers sometimes trap coatings automatically, but you must explicitly exclude the barcode area from any gloss, soft-touch, or foil finishes.

### Forgetting to Cover Old Barcodes

If you reuse stock boxes or apply a new UPC over an existing barcode, the old barcode must be completely covered. Amazon requires that any existing external barcodes on shipping boxes must be rendered unscannable to prevent processing errors ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a6a5bbb9-3d5e-4ec1-afb6-e1459dd810f6?postId=a6a5bbb9-3d5e-4ec1-afb6-e1459dd810f6)). Use a solid opaque label to cover the old code completely.

## FAQ

### What is the GS1 quiet zone?
The quiet zone is the clear, unprinted white space on the left and right sides of a barcode. Scanners need this blank margin to identify the start and end of the barcode data. Without enough clear space, the scanner cannot locate where the code begins or ends, resulting in a failed read.

### Can I print a barcode in red ink?
No, you should not print barcode bars in red or reddish colors like brown. Barcode scanners use red light, which makes red bars invisible to the scanner and prevents a clean scan. Always choose dark colors like black, dark blue, or dark green for the bars.

### Can I make a barcode shorter to fit my box design?
You should never truncate or shorten a barcode. UPC barcodes have a fixed relationship between height and width, and cutting the height can result in incorrect scanning at checkout. If you need a smaller code, regenerate the file in the exact size you need using a certified tool.

### Where should I place the barcode on my custom box?
For retail items, place the barcode in the lower right-hand section of the back of the package. Ensure the surface is flat, smooth, and away from folds, edges, or glossy coatings. Avoid placing the code over embossed logos or heavy texture lines that interfere with the laser.

### What color background is best for a barcode?
A white or light-colored background is best. If you do not print a background, the natural color of the paper box material serves as the background, provided it is light enough for the dark bars to stand out. Avoid dark or busy backgrounds that reduce contrast and interfere with the scan.
