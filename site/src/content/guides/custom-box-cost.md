Custom printed box cost is affected by size, quantity, board type, printing coverage, surface finish, inserts and shipping destination. The same artwork can have a very different unit price at 500 pieces and 5,000 pieces because setup and freight are spread across a different number of boxes.

## Main cost factors

Box size affects paper usage and shipping volume. Quantity affects setup cost per box. Finishes such as foil stamping, embossing, spot UV and soft-touch lamination add separate production steps.

| Cost factor | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Box size | Paper usage, carton packing volume and shipping cost |
| Board type | Strength, print appearance and unit cost |
| Quantity | Setup cost per box and production efficiency |
| Print coverage | Ink use, color control and production time |
| Inserts | Material, assembly work and packed volume |
| Finish effects | Extra setup for foil, UV, embossing or lamination |

## Freight matters

Packaging is bulky. For overseas buyers, shipping can be a major part of the landed cost. Always include destination country when asking for a quote.

Rigid boxes and assembled gift boxes take more space than flat folding cartons. Mailer boxes and shipping boxes may ship flat, but the carton size still matters. When comparing suppliers, ask whether the quote includes only product cost or also freight.

## Best way to compare quotes

Ask suppliers to quote the same size, material, quantity and finish. Otherwise, a cheaper quote may simply be using a thinner board or a simpler finish.

Use the same brief for each supplier:

- Box style and finished size
- Quantity
- Paper or board preference
- Printing requirements
- Finish effects
- Insert requirements
- Shipping destination

## How to reduce cost without making the box look cheap

Start with structure and material before adding finishes. A clean dieline, good paper choice and well-placed logo often matter more than adding every effect.

Practical ways to control cost:

- Use one box size for several similar products when possible.
- Keep foil stamping on the logo or one key detail.
- Avoid full inside printing unless it improves the unboxing experience.
- Choose a folding carton instead of a rigid box when shelf display is enough.
- Compare landed cost, not only unit price.

## FAQ

### Why does the price drop at higher quantities?

Setup work is shared across more boxes, and production becomes more efficient. That usually lowers unit cost.

### Is the cheapest quote the best quote?

Not always. Check board thickness, print method, finish, packing details and freight before comparing prices.
